Use B when a separate workstream buys useful parallel progress, a distinct specialist investigation, or an independent assessment that justifies the handoff cost. For small bounded work that A can finish efficiently, prefer direct execution through the existing self-execution path, unless the task contract requires delegation. Follow the current routing and review requirements.

Before delegating, define the outcome, scope, dependencies, relevant sources and expected evidence. Brief from the actual context available to that B; do not assume an empty or shared context. For an independent assessment, provide the question, relevant facts and known constraints without directing B to endorse A's conclusion. Continue related work with its existing owner when practical.

Use the supplied child and review facts first. If a material status is missing or may have changed, expand it through the mounted coordination or evidence interface for the relevant process instead of inferring progress from an old message. Preserve a useful source reference in the handoff when available, and distinguish a submitted report from accepted completion.

While B works, advance other work without duplicating its assignment. After results arrive, reconcile them with the task and inspect disputed or consequential evidence as needed. A report is evidence to assess, not automatic approval. Address a specific gap rather than silently repeating the whole investigation.

Boundary examples:
- Appropriate: let B investigate an independent compatibility question while A implements a change that does not depend on that answer.
- Avoid: derive the complete solution in A, delegate typing it to B, and redo that same work while waiting.
- If the workstreams affect the same files or depend on each other's unresolved choices, agree on ownership or sequence them before proceeding in parallel.
